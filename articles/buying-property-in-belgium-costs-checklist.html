BUYER'S GUIDE

Buying Property in Belgium: Costs and Checklist

Buying a home is about more than the asking price. A clear budget and careful document checks can help you compare properties with confidence.

Budget for the full purchase

Include the price, applicable registration duties or VAT, notary costs, mortgage-related charges, insurance, moving costs and any immediate renovation. Tax treatment depends on the region, the type of property and your circumstances. Ask a Belgian notary for a written estimate before making a binding commitment.

Compare Flanders, Brussels and Wallonia carefully

Property transaction rules and potential tax benefits can differ by region and may change. Do not assume a rate or exemption applies to every buyer. Confirm the current rules for the exact property and whether it will be your main residence.

Inspect the home and its documents

  • Request the energy performance certificate (EPC/PEB).
  • Ask about roof, heating, damp, insulation and electrical compliance.
  • Review planning information and permits where relevant.
  • For an apartment, review shared charges, planned works and owners' association records.
  • Compare the asking price with similar properties in the same neighbourhood.

Understand the offer

An accepted offer may have legal consequences. Clearly state the property, price, deadlines and any conditions, such as mortgage approval. Have a notary or qualified professional review unclear terms before you sign or commit.

Before you decide

Compare total cost, commute, local amenities, energy performance and likely maintenance—not just floor area. Visit at different times of day and verify listing details with the responsible seller or agent.
